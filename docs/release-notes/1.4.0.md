HSF 1.4.0 rips the music that waits for values from a game: the rhythm pieces of Super Mario Party Jamboree and Super Mario Party, Jamboree's star melody, and the sounds that pick a variant through the game's code. It also rips WarioWare: Get It Together!'s layered pieces in each state, with the reverbs that its microgames put on aux bus B. The player now runs the audio renderer's reverb. Clubhouse Games: 51 Worldwide Classics puts it on aux bus B in two of its games. Rips of the WarioWare games play at the levels in their sound sheets. `hsfrip` now reads Super Mario Party Jamboree's archives and the group files of Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury. The format gets three flag bits in version 1's descriptor and a fourth type of aux effect. HSF 1.3.0 and earlier refuse the rips that use them.

HSF rips Nintendo Switch sequenced music into a PSF-family format. It plays the music of NintendoWare sound archives with a model of the SDK's sound library and of the console's audio renderer. The player adds the effects that a game puts on the music, such as a reverb. HSF plays the sound of the Switch releases of the Super Mario Galaxy games, Pikmin and Pikmin 2 with models of the games' versions of JAudio. The release includes a ripper (`hsfrip`), a renderer (`hsfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. `docs/hsf.md` describes the format.

## New in 1.4.0

- **Super Mario Party Jamboree.** The game's Bezel Engine archives keep each asset's header in the asset table. `hsfrip` now reads that layout too. It used to read none of the game's sound archives.
- **Rhythm pieces.** The rhythm mode of Super Mario Party Jamboree and the Sound Stage of Super Mario Party play each piece beside a controller sequence. The game sets the tempo. The piece waits until the controller hands over to it at the end of a bar. Under some pieces, the controller plays a kick drum on each beat. The pieces of Super Mario Party used to rip silent. `hsfrip` now recognizes the sub-archives of both modes. A rip of such a piece starts the controller and the piece as the game does, with the tempo and the number that the game sets. The rip starts as the piece starts. A track that polls the game's variables no longer keeps the length analysis from finding a loop. The pieces that loop now loop in their rips.
- **Jamboree's star melody.** The star melody follows the beat and the chords of the board's music. The game plays it over one of the streams of the boards. The melody reads the stream's beats and chords through the game's code. `hsfrip` now rips the melody once over each board stream that the game's files hold. The player runs a model of the game's music sync. Each rip is named after the melody and the stream. The stream itself isn't part of the rip.
- **Variants that the game's code picks.** In Super Mario Party Jamboree and Mario Party Superstars, a sound can pick one of its variants through a user procedure. That's code of the game that draws a number and passes it to the sequence. In Mario Party Superstars, such a sound used to play another note. The player now runs the procedures of both games as the game's code does. Each rip starts from the state that the game's code sets up as the game starts.
- **Sounds that wait for the game's state.** Some sound effects play only once the game has written one of their variables as it starts them, such as the surface under a footstep. Others play only on some draws of the game's random numbers. The length analysis used to skip such effects as silent. It now tries the states that the game's code can give them. For Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury and some archives of Super Mario Party Jamboree, `hsfrip` contains the values that the game's code writes. An effect that draws a number and stays silent gets a later start, 1 to 400 frames on. The sound library steps its random generator once a frame. The model plays those frames before the output starts. The rip therefore starts with the sound.
- **Layered pieces in WarioWare: Get It Together!** Some of the game's microgames turn tracks of their music up and down as the microgame goes on: the guitar, the three spotlight dances, the windmill and its success music, the dance, and Super Wario Co's results. A rip used to play every track. A rip of such a piece now plays it as the game starts it. Each later state gets a rip too, under a name such as `BGM_P_Guitar 60 percent`. The state's tracks play from the start.
- **Reverbs on aux bus B in WarioWare: Get It Together!** Each microgame puts a reverb on aux bus B as it starts. The reverb stays there until the stage ends. The rips of a microgame now play with its reverb on bus B. The music of the stages and the other sequences of the game that send to bus B get three rips each: one with bus B empty, and one with each of the two reverbs, such as `BGM_T_Sport_SpeedUp Hall reverb`.
- **The renderer's reverb.** The player now runs the audio renderer's reverb, `nn::atk`'s `EffectReverb`. The model follows the renderer in WarioWare: Move It!'s `sdk` module, and `nn::atk`'s reverb in the code of Clubhouse Games: 51 Worldwide Classics. That game puts the reverb on aux bus B in two of its games, the piano and bowling. The rips of their sounds play with that reverb.
- **Levels from the sound sheets.** WarioWare: Get It Together! and WarioWare: Move It! keep each sound's level in their sound sheets, outside the sound archive. `hsfrip` now writes that level as the mini's `volume` tag. A rip used to play each piece at the archive's level. The length analysis judges the output at the level of the tag.
- **Group files in SARC archives.** Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury keeps the group files of its sound effects in SARC archives (`.sarc`) under other names. `hsfrip` found group files only by the names of their groups. It skipped every sequence of that sound archive. While a group is still missing, `hsfrip` now reads the group files in the SARC archives in the sound archive's folder too.
- **The Galaxy games' track states.** In both Super Mario Galaxy games, a piece of music that plays other tracks in a later state now gets a rip of each such state. The name of such a rip adds the numbers of the states in the game's table, such as `states 8 to 10`. In Super Mario Galaxy's three Comet Observatory pieces, two states each bring in one of two layers. Each layer gets a rip.
- **Pikmin 2's layers.** Each piece of Pikmin 2 with layers now gets a rip of each layer, such as `forest battle layer`. That rip plays the layer from the start, at the highest level that the game gives it. The layers of the bosses' music and of the two players' battle get rips too.
- **Pikmin's update.** `hsfrip` now reads Pikmin's settings from the `main` of the update, version 1.1.0, as well as from version 1.0.0. The two builds contain the same settings at other addresses. Their rips are the same.
- **Pikmin's cutscenes.** A cutscene can stop one of its sounds with a later event. The rip of such a sound used to play on until the cutscene finished. The rip now fades out over the time that the game's sound takes to stop, and ends with the game's sound.
- **Counted loops at the time limit.** When the length analysis reaches its time limit inside counted loops, it measures the sequence again with those loops endless. It used to make every counted loop of a track endless. It now makes only the outermost counted loop of each track endless. An inner loop then still ends in each pass of the outer loop. The game plays it that way.
- **The format.** Version 1's descriptor gets three flag bits. Bit 0 gives the volumes of a sound's tracks. Bit 1 gives scripted starts: writes of a sequence's variables and starts of other sounds at given frames, such as a controller beside its piece. A scripted start can take a sound from an add-on archive. Bit 2 names a game's handler of user procedures. Version 1 also gets a fourth type of aux effect, type 4 for the renderer's reverb. `docs/hsf.md` describes them. HSF 1.3.0 and earlier refuse such rips with a message. Descriptors without them are the same as before, byte for byte.
- **Playback.** The foobar2000 component and the XMPlay plugin now render three seconds ahead before playback starts, and after each seek. A track that renders faster than real time on average then plays through its slow seconds without gaps.
- **Time tags.** A time with an empty field, such as `fade=:05`, now reads as 0 for that field. A player used to ignore such a tag.
- **The libraries.** HSF now builds on ntdlib 3.2.0 and xsflib 1.8.0.

## Features

- **Ripping.** `hsfrip` rips a Switch game's files or a sound archive (`.bfsar`) into an `.hsflib` and a `.minihsf` for each sequence. The game's files are a folder or a Bezel Engine archive (`.bea`). The folder is the game's RomFS, once it's extracted from the game, or a folder that holds the RomFS. `hsfrip` reads the files inside a game's Bezel Engine archives too. It finds the group files that a game keeps outside its sound archive, in SARC archives too. For the Super Mario Galaxy games and Pikmin 2, `hsfrip` reads the game's JAudio files in the RomFS's `AudioRes` folder. For Pikmin 2, it also reads the game's list of its music and its track maps in the RomFS's `user` folder. For Pikmin, `hsfrip` reads the game's sound files in the RomFS's `SndData` folder and the settings in the ExeFS's `main`. Give it a folder that holds the RomFS in `romfs` and the ExeFS in `exefs`. The ripper plays each sound to work out its length and fade tags, and skips the sequences that make no sound. `--bgm` rips only the music. `hsfrip` also supports drag and drop.
- **The game's code.** Some games change their sound through their code. `hsfrip` gives a rip what the code of these games sets: the states of WarioWare: Get It Together!'s layered pieces, the controllers of the rhythm pieces in the Mario Party games, the beats and chords that Jamboree's star melody reads, and the values that some sound effects wait for. The player runs the user procedures of Super Mario Party Jamboree and Mario Party Superstars.
- **The sound library.** The model follows the version of `nn::atk` in WarioWare: Get It Together! (SDK 10.4.1): its sequence player, envelopes, LFOs, filters, voices and mixes, with the Switch's 5 ms frames.
- **The console's mixing.** The Switch mixes its voices in an audio renderer that runs in the system rather than in the game. HSF does the renderer's work in code: the voices' decoding, sample-rate conversion, filters, volume and mix ramps and depop, and the mixes. The code does the same arithmetic as the renderer's build for the CPU in games' `sdk` modules.
- **JAudio.** The Switch releases of the Super Mario Galaxy games, Pikmin and Pikmin 2 run JAudio natively. JAudio is the sound library of Nintendo's GameCube and Wii games. Each release links an emulator that runs JAudio's DSP program. HSF plays their rips on a model of each game's version of JAudio and of the emulation: JAudio2 for the Galaxy games, JAudio1 for Pikmin 2 and the C JAudio for Pikmin. The rips play with the levels and the FX lines that each game sets up.
- **Effects.** A game can put effects on the sound library's aux buses. HSF plays the I3DL2 reverb that a game runs on an aux bus. HSF also plays the audio renderer's delay, light limiter and reverb. HSF's I3DL2 reverb does the same arithmetic as Nintendo's reverb, bit for bit, and the aux bus's 20 ms round trip is kept. The delay, the light limiter and the renderer's reverb follow the renderer in WarioWare: Move It!'s `sdk` module (SDK 14.3). For a game on Bezel Engine, `hsfrip` takes the effects that the game puts on the aux buses, the voice count that its `boot.lua` gives the engine and the levels in its sound sheets.
- **Rendering.** `hsfplay` renders rips to WAV or FLAC at 48,000 Hz.
- **foobar2000.** The component plays `.minihsf` files using their length and fade tags. Tag editing, ReplayGain and endless playback are supported. A separate thread renders ahead of playback and keeps up to ten minutes of audio. That makes seeking within a track quick. A seek back past that audio starts from a saved state of the player.
- **XMPlay.** The plugin plays `.minihsf` files using their length and fade tags. Endless playback is supported. Like the component, the plugin renders ahead of playback, keeps up to ten minutes of audio and seeks back from saved states.

## Known limitations

- The model follows WarioWare: Get It Together!'s version of the sound library. A game with another version may play differently from the console.
- Of the effects a NintendoWare game can use, HSF plays the I3DL2 reverb on an aux bus and the renderer's delay, light limiter and reverb. The renderer's other effects aren't played. A NintendoWare game that isn't on Bezel Engine gets no effects from `hsfrip`.
- A Bezel Engine game's scenes can name another reverb for a stage. `hsfrip` gives every rip the default one on aux bus A.
- In a rip of a later state of a layered piece, the state's tracks play from the start. In the game, the tracks change as the microgame goes on.
- WarioWare: Get It Together! and WarioWare: Move It! can play a sound through other records of their sound sheets, at other levels. A rip takes the record named after the sound. Where the game varies a level at random, a rip takes the middle of the range.
- WarioWare: Move It! shortens its delay when the game speeds up. A rip has the delay at the game's normal speed.
- WarioWare: Move It! turns its output down to half of full scale on a TV. The console applies that level after the game's mix. A rip plays the mix at full scale. On a TV, a rip plays about 6 dB louder than the game.
- Super Mario Party Jamboree and Mario Party Superstars seed the generators of their user procedures from the clock. A rip can't know that seed. It plays the same variant every time. In the game, the variant changes from play to play.
- The rips of Jamboree's star melody leave out the stream that the melody plays over.
- For another game that plays its sound with JAudio2, `hsfrip` warns and takes JAudio2's defaults. Its rips may play differently from the game. `hsfrip` rips no other game that plays its sound with JAudio1 or the C JAudio.
- The Galaxy games play much of their music as streams. HSF doesn't play streams. Some pieces of music are layers that play beside a stream. The rip of such a piece holds the layer alone. A layer that starts silent is skipped. Some of the games' sounds stand for a pair of other sounds. A piece of music and a stream make one such pair. `hsfrip` leaves those sounds out.
- The Galaxy games change some settings as they go on: the levels of a scene, the effects of some of Super Mario Galaxy 2's stages, and the tracks that a piece of music plays. A rip has the settings that the game sets up as it starts and as each piece starts. A rip of a later track state plays the state's tracks from the start. The two layers of a Comet Observatory piece together get no rip.
- In the Galaxy games, sounds that play notes on banks that the game doesn't load are listed and skipped.
- The Galaxy games turn their output down on the console. The console applies that level after the game's mix. A rip plays the mix at full scale. On a TV, a rip plays about 9 dB louder than the game. On the console's speaker, it plays about 3 dB louder.
- Pikmin 2 plays the music of its caves with a conductor that picks the phrases at random. Each rip of a cave's piece plays it with one conductor. `hsfrip` rips every conductor of the game, those that the game never plays included. The README lists the ones that the game plays. A cave's piece never repeats. Its rip plays for 10 minutes and then fades. Each play of a rip draws the same phrases. The game draws others each time it plays the piece.
- Pikmin 2's files hold some pieces that the game never plays. `hsfrip` rips all of them but `result`. That piece makes no sound. Some pieces have no track map. Their rips play every track.
- Pikmin 2 fades some tracks of a piece in and out as the game goes on: the layers for events, treasure, enemies nearby, battles and the ground. A rip plays those layers muted. Each piece starts that way in the game. The rip of a layer plays it at its highest level from the start.
- Pikmin and Pikmin 2 play most effects at a place in the game's world. Their levels move as the camera and the sound's source move. A rip plays each effect at the listener. Some effects play until the game stops them. Their rips loop and then fade.
- `hsfrip` reads Pikmin's settings from the `main` of the game's version 1.0.0 or 1.1.0. For another build of `main`, or without it, `hsfrip` stops with a message.
- Pikmin never plays `d_end2`, and the bank file has no wave system for it. `hsfrip` leaves it out with a warning.
- Pikmin sets some effects as the game goes on: the formation's sound by how far the stick is pushed, and the crowd of Pikmin by its size. The formation's rip plays the sound with the stick pushed all the way. A Pikmin's cry as it's plucked is one of several. The rip of the pluck plays one of them.
- Pikmin's Switch release plays nothing for the crowd of Pikmin, or for the system's effect `SYSSE_JUNGLE`. Their notes play waves that the bank file lacks. `hsfrip` skips them as sounds that make no sound.
- A Pikmin cutscene plays its sounds at the points of its timeline. Each sound of a cutscene gets a separate rip.
- Pikmin and Pikmin 2 turn their output down on the console: to 0.3 of full scale on a TV, and to 0.6 on the console's speaker, on headphones and on a USB device. A rip plays the mix at full scale. On a TV, a rip plays about 10.5 dB louder than the game. On the console's speaker, it plays about 4.4 dB louder.
- In a rip of a JAudio game, a seek back past the audio that the component or the XMPlay plugin keeps plays the track again from its start.
- The output is stereo. Surround isn't played.
- Other sequences that depend on values the game sets play as they do before the game sets any. A sequence that draws random numbers takes the same path every time.
- Sounds that play until the game stops them (such as ambiences) get two loops and a fade if the ripper can detect their loops. Otherwise, they play until their notes end. The limit is ten minutes.
- Only sequences are ripped. Use tools such as vgmstream for BFSTM and BWAV streams. Wave sounds aren't ripped. Sequences whose data is in a group file that can't be found are skipped.
- `hsfrip` refuses the Wii U's sound archives. They have the Switch's extension (`.bfsar`) but the other byte order.
- On Windows 10 before version 1903, `hsfrip` and `hsfplay` can't open a path with characters outside the system's code page.
- Encrypted games (NSP, XCI and NCA files) aren't read.
