HSF 1.3.0 rips three more Switch games: WarioWare: Move It!, and Pikmin and Pikmin 2 from Pikmin 1+2. WarioWare: Move It! puts a delay and a light limiter on its sound. HSF now plays both of these effects of the console's audio renderer. Pikmin 2 plays its sound with JAudio1, and Pikmin with the C JAudio. JAudio1 and the C JAudio came before JAudio2, the version in the Super Mario Galaxy games. HSF plays the rips of the two Pikmin games on models of those two versions. These rips are version 2 of the format, with two new libraries. A fix changes some sound effects of the Super Mario Galaxy games. Other rips of NintendoWare sound archives are the same as 1.2.0's.

HSF rips Nintendo Switch sequenced music into a PSF-family format. It plays the music of NintendoWare sound archives with a model of the SDK's sound library and of the console's audio renderer. The player adds the effects that a game puts on the music, such as a reverb. HSF plays the sound of the Switch releases of the Super Mario Galaxy games, Pikmin and Pikmin 2 with models of the games' versions of JAudio. The release includes a ripper (`hsfrip`), a renderer (`hsfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. `docs/hsf.md` describes the format.

## New in 1.3.0

- **WarioWare: Move It!** `hsfrip` now rips WarioWare: Move It! with the effects that the game puts on the aux buses. As the game starts, it puts a delay on aux bus A, an I3DL2 reverb on aux bus B and a light limiter on aux bus C. The game keeps the three effects in its sound effect list. That list has another form than WarioWare: Get It Together!'s. `hsfrip` reads the new form and puts the three effects on every rip of the game. HSF 1.2.0 and earlier refuse the game's rips with a message.
- **The renderer's delay and light limiter.** The player now runs two of the audio renderer's effects: the delay and the light limiter. The models follow the renderer's code in WarioWare: Move It!'s `sdk` module (SDK 14.3) and `nn::atk`'s `EffectDelay` and `EffectLightLimiter`. A sound's length includes the delay's echoes.
- **Pikmin 2.** Pikmin 2's Switch release, in Pikmin 1+2, plays its sound with JAudio1, the version of JAudio before JAudio2. Like the Galaxy games, the release runs JAudio natively and links an emulator that runs JAudio's DSP program. `hsfrip` now rips the game. Give it the game's RomFS folder. The ripper finds the game's sound files in the `AudioRes` folder. The `user` folder beside it holds the game's list of its music and its track maps. `hsfrip` reads them too. Each piece of music and each sound effect gets a rip. `--bgm` rips the music. The list of the music gives the volumes of the pieces and of the effects. The track maps give the layers of each piece. `hsfrip` holds the rest of each piece's settings from the game's code. The game plays the music of its caves with conductors that pick the phrases at random. `hsfrip` rips each cave's piece once with each of its conductors. Each of those rips has the conductor's name.
- **A model of JAudio1.** The player plays Pikmin 2's rips on a model of JAudio1 and of the game's sound code above it. The model covers JAudio1's sequences, instrument banks, channels, envelopes and LFOs. The model also covers the game's four classes of music, the cave music's conductors and the way the game starts its effects. The model of the DSP program now plays the program's oscillators too. Some of Pikmin 2's sound effects play them. The length analysis plays each sound on the same model.
- **Pikmin.** Pikmin's Switch release, also in Pikmin 1+2, plays its sound with the C JAudio (Jac), the version of JAudio before JAudio1. `hsfrip` now rips the game. Give it a folder that holds the game's RomFS in `romfs` and its ExeFS in `exefs`. The ripper finds the game's sound files in the RomFS's `SndData` folder. The game keeps the rest of its sound settings in its code, the ExeFS's `main`. `hsfrip` reads them from the `main` of the game's version 1.0.0. They include the index of the sequence archive, the settings of the music and the effects, the FX lines and the cutscenes' table. Each piece of music, each piece of the cutscenes' music and each sound effect gets a rip. Each sound of a cutscene gets a rip too. The rip of such a sound ends where the game finishes the cutscene. `--bgm` rips the two kinds of music.
- **A model of the C JAudio.** The player plays Pikmin's rips on a model of the C JAudio and of the game's sound code. The model covers the C JAudio's sequences, instrument banks, wave scenes, channels, envelopes and LFOs. The model also covers the ways in which the game's code starts the music, the effects and the cutscenes' sounds through the sequences' ports. The length analysis plays each sound on the same model.
- **The format.** Version 1's descriptor gets two more types of aux effect: 2 for the renderer's delay and 3 for its light limiter. Version 2's descriptor gets two more libraries: library 2 for Pikmin 2's JAudio1 and library 3 for Pikmin's C JAudio. `docs/hsf.md` describes them. HSF 1.2.0 and earlier refuse such rips with a message. Descriptors without them are the same as before, byte for byte.
- **The Galaxy games' waves.** In the Super Mario Galaxy games, a wave can sit in several groups of a wave system. Each group's wave archive holds a copy of the wave, at another offset. The player took the offset of one copy and read it from another group's archive. Some sound effects of both games therefore played the wrong samples. Some of Mario's voice clips in Super Mario Galaxy were among them. The player now reads each wave from the right archive. The music is unchanged. The fix also changes the lengths of some of those effects. Rips made with HSF 1.2.0 play the right samples in 1.3.0's players. A new rip also gets the new lengths.
- **hsfrip's help.** `hsfrip --help` now says what `--bgm` takes from a JAudio game: the sounds that the game plays as music. The help also says that without the length analysis, only a sound archive's sequences at a volume of 0 are skipped.
- **The libraries.** HSF now builds on ntdlib 1.14.0, with xsflib 1.6.0.

## Features

- **Ripping.** `hsfrip` rips a Switch game's files or a sound archive (`.bfsar`) into an `.hsflib` and a `.minihsf` for each sequence. The game's files are a folder or a Bezel Engine archive (`.bea`). The folder is the game's RomFS, once it's extracted from the game, or a folder that holds the RomFS. `hsfrip` reads the files inside a game's Bezel Engine archives too. It finds the group files that a game keeps outside its sound archive. For the Super Mario Galaxy games and Pikmin 2, `hsfrip` reads the game's JAudio files in the RomFS's `AudioRes` folder. For Pikmin 2, it also reads the game's list of its music and its track maps in the RomFS's `user` folder. For Pikmin, `hsfrip` reads the game's sound files in the RomFS's `SndData` folder and the settings in the ExeFS's `main`. Give it a folder that holds the RomFS in `romfs` and the ExeFS in `exefs`. The ripper plays each sound to work out its length and fade tags, and skips the sequences that make no sound. `--bgm` rips only the music. `hsfrip` also supports drag and drop.
- **The sound library.** The model follows the version of `nn::atk` in WarioWare: Get It Together! (SDK 10.4.1): its sequence player, envelopes, LFOs, filters, voices and mixes, with the Switch's 5 ms frames.
- **The console's mixing.** The Switch mixes its voices in an audio renderer that runs in the system rather than in the game. HSF does the renderer's work in code: the voices' decoding, sample-rate conversion, filters, volume and mix ramps and depop, and the mixes. The code does the same arithmetic as the renderer's build for the CPU in games' `sdk` modules.
- **JAudio.** The Switch releases of the Super Mario Galaxy games, Pikmin and Pikmin 2 run JAudio natively. JAudio is the sound library of Nintendo's GameCube and Wii games. Each release links an emulator that runs JAudio's DSP program. HSF plays their rips on a model of each game's version of JAudio and of the emulation: JAudio2 for the Galaxy games, JAudio1 for Pikmin 2 and the C JAudio for Pikmin. The rips play with the levels and the FX lines that each game sets up.
- **Effects.** A game can put effects on the sound library's aux buses. HSF plays the I3DL2 reverb that a game runs on an aux bus. HSF also plays the audio renderer's delay and light limiter. HSF's reverb does the same arithmetic as Nintendo's reverb, bit for bit, and the aux bus's 20 ms round trip is kept. The delay and the light limiter follow the renderer in WarioWare: Move It!'s `sdk` module (SDK 14.3). For a game on Bezel Engine, `hsfrip` takes the effects that the game puts on the aux buses, and the voice count its `boot.lua` gives the engine.
- **Rendering.** `hsfplay` renders rips to WAV or FLAC at 48,000 Hz.
- **foobar2000.** The component plays `.minihsf` files using their length and fade tags. Tag editing, ReplayGain and endless playback are supported. A separate thread renders ahead of playback and keeps up to ten minutes of audio. That makes seeking within a track quick. A seek back past that audio starts from a saved state of the player.
- **XMPlay.** The plugin plays `.minihsf` files using their length and fade tags. Endless playback is supported. Like the component, the plugin renders ahead of playback, keeps up to ten minutes of audio and seeks back from saved states.

## Known limitations

- The model follows WarioWare: Get It Together!'s version of the sound library. A game with another version may play differently from the console.
- Of the effects a NintendoWare game can use, HSF plays the I3DL2 reverb on an aux bus and the renderer's delay and light limiter. The renderer's other effects, such as its reverb, aren't played. A NintendoWare game that isn't on Bezel Engine gets no effects from `hsfrip`.
- A Bezel Engine game's scenes can name another reverb for a stage. `hsfrip` gives every rip the default one.
- WarioWare: Move It! shortens its delay when the game speeds up. A rip has the delay at the game's normal speed.
- WarioWare: Move It! turns its output down to half of full scale on a TV. The console applies that level after the game's mix. A rip plays the mix at full scale. On a TV, a rip plays about 6 dB louder than the game.
- For another game that plays its sound with JAudio2, `hsfrip` warns and takes JAudio2's defaults. Its rips may play differently from the game. `hsfrip` rips no other game that plays its sound with JAudio1 or the C JAudio.
- The Galaxy games play much of their music as streams. HSF doesn't play streams. Some pieces of music are layers that play beside a stream. The rip of such a piece holds the layer alone. A layer that starts silent is skipped. Some of the games' sounds stand for a pair of other sounds. A piece of music and a stream make one such pair. `hsfrip` leaves those sounds out.
- The Galaxy games change some settings as they go on: the levels of a scene, the effects of some of Super Mario Galaxy 2's stages, and the tracks that a piece of music plays. A rip has the settings that the game sets up as it starts and as each piece starts.
- In the Galaxy games, sounds that play notes on banks that the game doesn't load are listed and skipped.
- The Galaxy games turn their output down on the console. The console applies that level after the game's mix. A rip plays the mix at full scale. On a TV, a rip plays about 9 dB louder than the game. On the console's speaker, it plays about 3 dB louder.
- Pikmin 2 plays the music of its caves with a conductor that picks the phrases at random. Each rip of a cave's piece plays it with one conductor. `hsfrip` rips every conductor of the game, those that the game never plays included. The README lists the ones that the game plays. A cave's piece never repeats. Its rip plays for 10 minutes and then fades. Each play of a rip draws the same phrases. The game draws others each time it plays the piece.
- Pikmin 2's files hold some pieces that the game never plays. `hsfrip` rips all of them but `result`. That piece makes no sound. Some pieces have no track map. Their rips play every track.
- Pikmin 2 fades some tracks of a piece in and out as the game goes on: the layers for events, treasure, enemies nearby, battles and the ground. A rip plays those layers muted. Each piece starts that way in the game.
- Pikmin and Pikmin 2 play most effects at a place in the game's world. Their levels move as the camera and the sound's source move. A rip plays each effect at the listener. Some effects play until the game stops them. Their rips loop and then fade.
- `hsfrip` reads Pikmin's settings from the `main` of the game's version 1.0.0. For another build of `main`, or without it, `hsfrip` stops with a message.
- Pikmin never plays `d_end2`, and the bank file has no wave system for it. `hsfrip` leaves it out with a warning.
- Pikmin sets some effects as the game goes on: the formation's sound by how far the stick is pushed, and the crowd of Pikmin by its size. The formation's rip plays the sound with the stick pushed all the way. A Pikmin's cry as it's plucked is one of several. The rip of the pluck plays one of them.
- Pikmin's Switch release plays nothing for the crowd of Pikmin, or for the system's effect `SYSSE_JUNGLE`. Their notes play waves that the bank file lacks. `hsfrip` skips them as sounds that make no sound.
- A Pikmin cutscene plays its sounds at the points of its timeline. Each sound of a cutscene gets a separate rip.
- A Pikmin cutscene can stop one of its sounds with a later event. The rip of such a sound plays on until the cutscene finishes.
- Pikmin and Pikmin 2 turn their output down on the console: to 0.3 of full scale on a TV, and to 0.6 on the console's speaker, on headphones and on a USB device. A rip plays the mix at full scale. On a TV, a rip plays about 10.5 dB louder than the game. On the console's speaker, it plays about 4.4 dB louder.
- In a rip of a JAudio game, a seek back past the audio that the component or the XMPlay plugin keeps plays the track again from its start.
- The output is stereo. Surround isn't played.
- A sequence that depends on values the game sets plays as it does before the game sets any. A sequence that draws random numbers takes the same path every time.
- Sounds that play until the game stops them (such as ambiences) get two loops and a fade if the ripper can detect their loops. Otherwise, they play until their notes end. The limit is ten minutes.
- Only sequences are ripped. Use tools such as vgmstream for BFSTM and BWAV streams. Wave sounds aren't ripped. Sequences whose data is in a group file that can't be found are skipped.
- `hsfrip` refuses the Wii U's sound archives. They have the Switch's extension (`.bfsar`) but the other byte order.
- On Windows 10 before version 1903, `hsfrip` and `hsfplay` can't open a path with characters outside the system's code page.
- Encrypted games (NSP, XCI and NCA files) aren't read.
