HSF 1.2.0 rips the Switch releases of Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2. These games play their sound with JAudio2. JAudio2 is the sound library of Nintendo's GameCube and Wii games. HSF plays their rips on a model of JAudio2 and of the emulation of its DSP program that the games run. These rips are version 2 of the format. Rips of NintendoWare sound archives are unchanged from 1.1.4.

HSF rips Nintendo Switch sequenced music into a PSF-family format. It plays the music of NintendoWare sound archives with a model of the SDK's sound library and of the console's audio renderer. The player adds the reverb that a game puts on the music. The release includes a ripper (`hsfrip`), a renderer (`hsfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. `docs/hsf.md` describes the format.

## New in 1.2.0

- **Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2.** The Switch releases of the two games run JAudio2 natively. They link an emulator that runs JAudio's DSP program. `hsfrip` now rips both games. Give it the game's RomFS folder. The ripper finds the game's JAudio files in the `AudioRes` folder. Each sound that plays a sequence gets a rip. That covers the music and the sound effects. `--bgm` rips the music. The rips play with the levels, the FX lines and the states of the tracks that the game's code sets up. HSF holds those settings for the two games. HSF recognises each game by its files. Super Mario Galaxy 2's code also sets the volumes of its music from a table in the game's files. `hsfrip` reads that table.
- **A model of JAudio2.** The player plays these rips on a model of JAudio2 and of the emulation of its DSP program. The model covers JAudio2's sequences, instrument banks, channels, envelopes and LFOs. It also covers the emulation's channels, mixers, FX lines and output. The model follows the arithmetic of the games' code. Like the games, the player converts the DSP's output from 32 kHz to 48 kHz. The length analysis plays each sound on the same model.
- **Version 2 of the format.** A rip of a Galaxy game is version 2 of the format. Its descriptor holds JAudio's settings. `docs/hsf.md` describes version 2. HSF 1.1.4 and earlier refuse version 2 rips with a message. Rips of NintendoWare sound archives stay version 1.
- **The libraries.** HSF now builds on ntdlib 1.13.0, with xsflib 1.6.0.

## Features

- **Ripping.** `hsfrip` rips a Switch game's files or a sound archive (`.bfsar`) into an `.hsflib` and a `.minihsf` for each sequence. The game's files are a folder or a Bezel Engine archive (`.bea`). The folder is the game's RomFS, once it's extracted from the game, or a folder that holds the RomFS. `hsfrip` reads the files inside a game's Bezel Engine archives too. It finds the group files that a game keeps outside its sound archive. For Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2, `hsfrip` reads the game's JAudio files in the RomFS's `AudioRes` folder. The ripper plays each sound to work out its length and fade tags, and skips the sequences that make no sound. `--bgm` rips only the music. `hsfrip` also supports drag and drop.
- **The sound library.** The model follows the version of `nn::atk` in WarioWare: Get It Together! (SDK 10.4.1): its sequence player, envelopes, LFOs, filters, voices and mixes, with the Switch's 5 ms frames.
- **The console's mixing.** The Switch mixes its voices in an audio renderer that runs in the system rather than in the game. HSF does the renderer's work in code: the voices' decoding, sample-rate conversion, filters, volume and mix ramps and depop, and the mixes. The code does the same arithmetic as the renderer's build for the CPU in games' `sdk` modules.
- **JAudio.** The Switch releases of the Super Mario Galaxy games play their sound with JAudio2 and an emulation of its DSP program. HSF plays their rips on a model of both, with the levels and the FX lines that the game's code sets up.
- **Effects.** A game can put effects on the sound library's aux buses. HSF plays the I3DL2 reverb that a game runs on an aux bus. HSF's reverb does the same arithmetic as Nintendo's reverb, bit for bit, and the aux bus's 20 ms round trip is kept. For a game on Bezel Engine, `hsfrip` takes the reverb its scenes use, and the voice count its `boot.lua` gives the engine.
- **Rendering.** `hsfplay` renders rips to WAV or FLAC at 48,000 Hz.
- **foobar2000.** The component plays `.minihsf` files using their length and fade tags. Tag editing, ReplayGain and endless playback are supported. A separate thread renders ahead of playback and keeps up to ten minutes of audio. That makes seeking within a track quick. A seek back past that audio starts from a saved state of the player.
- **XMPlay.** The plugin plays `.minihsf` files using their length and fade tags. Endless playback is supported. Like the component, the plugin renders ahead of playback, keeps up to ten minutes of audio and seeks back from saved states.

## Known limitations

- The model follows WarioWare: Get It Together!'s version of the sound library. A game with another version may play differently from the console.
- Of the effects a NintendoWare game can use, only the I3DL2 reverb on an aux bus is played. The renderer's reverb and delay effects aren't. A NintendoWare game that isn't on Bezel Engine gets no reverb from `hsfrip`.
- A Bezel Engine game's scenes can name another reverb for a stage. `hsfrip` gives every rip the default one.
- For a JAudio game other than the two Galaxy games, `hsfrip` warns and takes JAudio2's defaults. Its rips may play differently from the game.
- The Galaxy games play much of their music as streams. HSF doesn't play streams. Some pieces of music are layers that play beside a stream. The rip of such a piece holds the layer alone. A layer that starts silent is skipped. Some of the games' sounds stand for a pair of other sounds. A piece of music and a stream make one such pair. `hsfrip` leaves those sounds out.
- The Galaxy games change some settings as they go on: the levels of a scene, the effects of some of Super Mario Galaxy 2's stages, and the tracks that a piece of music plays. A rip has the settings that the game sets up as it starts and as each piece starts.
- In the Galaxy games, sounds that play notes on banks that the game doesn't load are listed and skipped.
- The Galaxy games turn their output down on the console. The console applies that level after the game's mix. A rip plays the mix at full scale. On a TV, a rip plays about 9 dB louder than the game. On the console's speaker, it plays about 3 dB louder.
- In a rip of a Galaxy game, a seek back past the audio that the component or the XMPlay plugin keeps plays the track again from its start.
- The output is stereo. Surround isn't played.
- A sequence that depends on values the game sets plays as it does before the game sets any. A sequence that draws random numbers takes the same path every time.
- Sounds that play until the game stops them (such as ambiences) get two loops and a fade if the ripper can detect their loops. Otherwise, they play until their notes end. The limit is ten minutes.
- Only sequences are ripped. Use tools such as vgmstream for BFSTM and BWAV streams. Wave sounds aren't ripped. Sequences whose data is in a group file that can't be found are skipped.
- `hsfrip` refuses the Wii U's sound archives. They have the Switch's extension (`.bfsar`) but the other byte order.
- On Windows 10 before version 1903, `hsfrip` and `hsfplay` can't open a path with characters outside the system's code page.
- Encrypted games (NSP, XCI and NCA files) aren't read.
